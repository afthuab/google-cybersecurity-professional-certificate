# Cybersecurity Fundamentals

## What is cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the work of protecting computers, networks, applications, and data from unauthorised access, damage, or disruption. It uses technology, policies, and good security practices to reduce risk.

## Why does it matter?

Most organisations rely on digital systems to store information and deliver services. A security incident can expose personal data, interrupt business, cause financial loss, or damage trust.

## What needs protection?

| Asset | Example |
|---|---|
| Devices | Laptops, phones, and servers |
| Software | Operating systems and business applications |
| Data | Customer records and financial information |
| Networks | Office networks, Wi-Fi, and VPNs |
| Accounts | Employee and administrator accounts |

## Common threats

- **Malware:** Software designed to harm a device, steal data, or gain access.
- **Phishing:** A deceptive message designed to make someone share information or take an unsafe action.
- **Ransomware:** Malware that blocks access to data or systems, often by encrypting files, and demands payment.
- **Social engineering:** Manipulating people into revealing information or doing something unsafe.
- **Denial of service:** Making a service unavailable by overwhelming it or exhausting its resources.
- **Insider threat:** A risk caused by someone with legitimate access, either deliberately or by mistake.

## Cybersecurity, information security, and network security

- **Cybersecurity** focuses on protecting digital systems and information from cyber threats.
- **Information security** protects information in any form, including digital and printed information.
- **Network security** protects network devices, connections, and traffic.

These areas overlap, but they do not mean exactly the same thing.

## Example

An employee receives an email asking them to reset their work password through an unfamiliar link. If the employee enters their password on a fake site, an attacker may use it to access company systems. Email filtering, multi-factor authentication, user awareness, and login monitoring can help reduce this risk.

## Key point

Cybersecurity is not just about installing antivirus software. It involves protecting people, devices, accounts, networks, and data through several layers of security.
