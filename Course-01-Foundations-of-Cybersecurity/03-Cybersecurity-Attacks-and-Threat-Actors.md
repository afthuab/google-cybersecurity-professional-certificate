# Cybersecurity Attacks and Threat Actors

## Common attack types

| Attack | What it means |
|---|---|
| Phishing | A fake message tricks someone into sharing information or clicking a link. |
| Malware | Malicious software is used to harm a system or gain access. |
| Ransomware | An attacker blocks access to data or systems and demands payment. |
| Brute force | Many password guesses are tried to gain access. |
| Denial of service (DoS) | A service is overwhelmed or its resources are exhausted. |
| Distributed denial of service (DDoS) | Many systems send traffic to overwhelm a target. |
| Man-in-the-middle | An attacker intercepts or changes communication between parties. |
| SQL injection | Unsafe application input is used to interfere with database queries. |
| Social engineering | People are manipulated into taking an unsafe action. |

## Threat actors

A threat actor is a person or group that may carry out an attack.

| Threat actor | Possible motivation |
|---|---|
| Cybercriminals | Money, fraud, or selling stolen data |
| Nation-state groups | Espionage or strategic goals |
| Hacktivists | Political or social causes |
| Insiders | Revenge, personal gain, or accidental misuse |
| Opportunistic attackers | Trying exposed systems or using available tools |

These categories can overlap. Motivation alone does not prove who carried out an attack.

## Why attacks happen

Common motivations include financial gain, data theft, espionage, disruption, political goals, and personal reasons.

## What analysts may look for

- Repeated failed logins
- Unexpected administrative activity
- Suspicious email attachments
- Unusual outbound network connections
- New or unexpected processes
- Large or unusual data transfers

One indicator by itself does not always prove an attack. Analysts compare evidence from different sources before reaching a conclusion.

## Key point

Knowing common attack methods helps an analyst understand what an alert may mean and what evidence to check next.
