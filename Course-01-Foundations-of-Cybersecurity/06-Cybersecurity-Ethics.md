# Cybersecurity Ethics

Cybersecurity professionals may have access to private data, business systems, and investigation records. They need to use that access carefully and only for approved work.

## Important principles

- **Confidentiality:** Do not share information with people who are not authorised to see it.
- **Privacy:** Handle personal information only when there is a valid reason and permission.
- **Integrity:** Keep records accurate and do not alter evidence.
- **Authorisation:** Do not test or access systems without permission.
- **Accountability:** Record important actions and follow organisational procedures.
- **Responsible disclosure:** Report vulnerabilities through an appropriate channel.

## Example 1: Unapproved access

An analyst notices that an employee has accessed records unrelated to their job.

The analyst should avoid accessing or sharing the records unnecessarily, preserve relevant evidence according to policy, document the concern, and report it through the approved process.

## Example 2: Finding a vulnerability

A researcher discovers a weakness on a company's website.

They should not exploit it further or access private data. They should use the company's security contact or vulnerability disclosure process to report the issue.

## Key point

Technical access does not mean permission to do anything. Ethical security work must stay within the approved scope and respect privacy.
