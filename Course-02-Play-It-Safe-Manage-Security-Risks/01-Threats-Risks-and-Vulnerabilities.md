# Threats, Risks, and Vulnerabilities

## 1. Key terms

These terms are related, but they do not mean the same thing.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | Something valuable that an organization needs to protect | Customer database |
| Threat | Something that could cause harm | Ransomware attacker |
| Vulnerability | A weakness that could be exploited | An unpatched server |
| Risk | The possibility of loss when a threat exploits a weakness | A server may be compromised and customer data exposed |
| Impact | The damage or consequence if an event occurs | Downtime, financial loss, or data exposure |

## 2. Common threats

- **Malware:** Software designed to damage systems, steal information, or gain unauthorized access.
- **Phishing:** Deceptive messages used to trick people into sharing information or taking unsafe actions.
- **Insider threat:** Risk caused by someone with authorized access, whether intentional or accidental.
- **Password attacks:** Attempts to guess, reuse, or steal account credentials.
- **Denial-of-service:** Attempts to make a system or service unavailable.
- **Social engineering:** Manipulating people into revealing information or performing an action.
- **Physical threats:** Theft, damage, or unauthorized physical access to equipment.

## 3. Common vulnerabilities

- Outdated software and missing security patches.
- Weak or reused passwords.
- Unnecessary user permissions.
- Misconfigured firewalls or cloud storage.
- Unprotected sensitive data.
- Poor backup and recovery practices.
- Lack of security awareness training.

## 4. Why businesses care about risk

A security incident can affect more than computers. It can interrupt services, expose customer information, cause financial loss, damage an organization's reputation, and create legal or regulatory problems.

## 5. What an entry-level analyst should look for

An analyst may notice repeated failed logins, unusual account activity, unexpected software, suspicious email reports, alerts from endpoint tools, or unusual network connections. These are clues to investigate, not proof of an attack by themselves.

## 6. Example

A small company stores customer information on a server that has not received security updates. The unpatched software is a vulnerability. An attacker attempting to exploit it is a threat. The possibility of unauthorized access and data exposure is a risk. The potential loss of customer trust and business disruption is the impact.

## 7. Key point

A useful risk assessment connects the asset, threat, vulnerability, likelihood, and possible impact. This helps an organization decide which issues need attention first.
